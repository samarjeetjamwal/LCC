1 What is a distributor credit score?

A distributor credit score is a numerical rating that estimates the risk of giving trade credit to a distributor. It helps you decide:

  • Whether to extend credit to a distributor
  • How much credit limit to give
  • Payment terms: prepaid, 15 / 30 / 60 days
  • Whether to increase, reduce or block credit
  • Which distributors need collection follow‑up
  • Which distributors may default or pay late

Common in FMCG, pharma, building materials, electronics, agro‑inputs and B2B distribution.

ScoreRisk CategoryDecision
80–100Low riskHigher limit, 30–60 days
60–79Medium riskModerate limit, 15–30 days
40–59High riskLow limit, partial advance
Below 40Very high riskPrepaid only / reject

The score can be based on:

  • Financial strength & payment history
  • Order behaviour & outstanding balances
  • Age of business & market reputation
  • Bank statements, collateral / guarantees
  • External data: legal cases, news, credit bureau

4 Types of credit‑scoring models

Model 1 — Application scorecard (new distributors)

  • Inputs: registration, years in business, owners, bank & trade references, financials, requested limit, territory, warehouse capacity
  • Output: approve/reject, initial limit, terms

Model 2 — Behavioral scorecard (existing distributors)

  • Inputs: payment history, days to pay, overdue invoices, order frequency/size trend, returns, disputes, utilization, outstanding
  • Output: probability of late payment / default, limit increase or decrease

Model 3 — Probability of Default (PD) model

Predicts chance of severe overdue in next 6–12 months.

5 Data needed for the model

A. Distributor master data

  • Name, legal entity, owners, registration & tax ID, address
  • Years in business, type, coverage area, warehouse capacity, employees, outlets served

B. Financial data

  • Revenue, profitability, working capital, debt levels, bank statements, audited financials, tax compliance, collateral

C. Transaction data

  • Invoice history, order frequency & value, product mix, returns, discounts, delivery performance, credit notes, disputes

D. Payment data

  • Due date, payment date, days past due, overdue amount, partial payments, promises, collection notes, write‑offs

E. External data

  • Credit bureau score, legal judgments, bankruptcy records, news sentiment, industry & regional risk

7 Simple rule‑based model

Rule 1 (New distributor): age below 1 year → max credit = 0.5 × avg monthly purchase; terms = advance or 7 days.
Rule 2 (Good payer): on‑time rate above 90% and zero overdue → increase limit 10–20%.
Rule 3 (High utilization): above 90% for 3 months → no increase; review capacity.
Rule 4 (Declining sales): purchases down more than 30% in 3 months → reduce limit 20%.
Rule 5 (Severe overdue): any invoice above 60 days overdue → credit hold until collection action.

14 Risks when using AI

1. Bias: may unfairly disadvantage regions, sizes or business types.
2. Poor data quality: wrong invoices/payments → wrong scores.
3. Overfitting: good on old data, fails on new distributors.
4. No explainability: credit teams must understand rejections/limits.
5. Economic shifts: model trained in good times fails in stress periods.
6. Over‑reliance: large limits still need human judgment.

15 Implementation roadmap (4 phases)

Phase 1 — Rule‑based scorecard

  • Age, payment history, outstanding, utilization, documents, references → basic score, limit, workflow

Phase 2 — Statistical scorecard

  • Use history to weight factors (payment history & utilization usually strongest) → better segmentation

Phase 3 — AI / ML model

  • Predict late payment, PD, expected loss; optimize limits; early warnings; dynamic limits

Phase 4 — Continuous monitoring

  • Bad debt by band, model drift, overrides, collection performance, profitability → model stays reliable

22 Summary

A distributor credit‑scoring model makes credit decisions faster, safer and consistent.

  • Start simple: scorecard on payment history, age, financials, sales behaviour, utilization, external risk.
  • Then add AI/ML: late‑payment & default probability, expected loss, optimal limits, early warnings.
  • AI benefits: pattern finding, document automation, limit recommendations, explanations, collections, portfolio monitoring.
Best approach: start simple → use data you already have → validate against actual bad debt → gradually move to AI‑driven dynamic credit decisions.
